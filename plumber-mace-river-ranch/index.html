What public sources say about Mace River Ranch

One listing source describes Mace River Ranch as about 220 homes on lots of 0.26 to 0.78 acre, with The Estates at the western end up to 3.9 acres and gated. It reports 12 lakes, a pool, a clubhouse, a park and 2.5 miles of walking paths.

A caution on the numbers.

Lot sizes, home counts and build years here are listing-site figures. County records can differ, and your HOA can tell you which ones apply to your phase.

Large lots mean long pipe runs

On a lot of half an acre or more, the water service line from the meter to the house is often longer than on a town lot. That is general plumbing knowledge. A longer run gives a leak more room to hide, and a wet patch in the yard may sit far from the house.

When we look at a yard leak we start at the meter, so we know whether water is escaping before or after the house shutoff. If you suspect a buried line, see water line repair for how we decide between digging and other options.

Which pipe is leaking in a wet yard

This neighborhood is reported to have pressurized irrigation, so a wet spot can come from irrigation lines, the house water line or surface water. The table shows how we sort them.

Sorting a wet yard on a large lot.
What you see. Likely first suspect. First check.
Wet spot only when sprinklers run. Irrigation line or valve. Run the zone and watch the spot.
Wet spot all the time, meter moves. House water service line. Meter test with all fixtures off.
Soft ground after heavy rain only. Surface water, not plumbing. Look at grading and downspouts.
Hot water bill jump, no wet spot. Leak inside the house. Check heater, slab and fixtures.

The U.S. EPA meter test is simple: read the meter, use no water for two hours, then read it again. If the dial moved, water is leaking somewhere. Our leak detection page covers the tools we use next.

Newer homes and the water heater clock

Sample listings here were built from 2015 to 2019. The Department of Energy puts storage tanks at 10 to 15 years, so many original heaters are in the middle of their range. A middle-aged tank is a good time to learn the label date and locate the shutoff.

If you want to plan ahead, our water heater replacement page lays out how we compare repair and replacement. We cannot tell the age of your heater without reading its label.

Utilities and permits to ask about

The listing source does not name the water or sewer provider for this neighborhood. In Eagle, ID, water can come from Veolia Water Idaho, the City of Eagle or a well, and the Eagle Sewer District serves sewer. Your bill will say which applies, and you should confirm before any digging.

Plumbing permits in Idaho come from the state DOPL, not the city. For the wider area we serve, see our Eagle service area page.
