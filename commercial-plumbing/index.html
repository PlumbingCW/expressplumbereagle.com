When a floor drain overflow is an emergency

A floor drain that gurgles, slows or bubbles after hours often points to a blocked line downstream, not just the drain itself. If other fixtures also drain slowly, the problem is probably the main branch.

What to do before we arrive at your business.

Stop using sinks, dishwashers and mop sinks that feed the same line. Close the shutoff valve to any fixture that is still running, and block off the wet floor so nobody slips.

Move stock and electrical cords off the floor, and take photos of the water and the damage. Do not pour drain chemicals into a backed-up line, because the next person who opens the drain can be splashed.

Night and weekend calls go to the same line, where same-day appointments are offered when available. No arrival time is promised, so tell the dispatcher what is overflowing and where the shutoff is.

What commercial plumbing service means here, and what it does not

We are honest about the size of work we take. This is service-call plumbing for small commercial spaces and property managers, such as restaurants, retail suites, offices and small rental buildings. It is not large construction work.

One difference from a house is the permit. The Idaho homeowner permit rule covers a person's own residence and does not apply to commercial buildings. Commercial plumbing work in Ada County needs a licensed plumber, and permit rules vary, so ask DOPL at 208-334-3233 if you are unsure.

Grease trap cleaning and what hauling means

A grease trap catches fat and food solids before they reach the sewer. When it fills, the grease passes through or backs up into the kitchen.

We open the trap, clean the baffles and outlet, and check the inlet and outlet lines. Full pumping and hauling of trap contents is usually done by a grease hauler with a tank truck, and we tell you on the call if your job needs one. Ask your landlord or the Eagle Sewer District about any grease rules that apply to your space.

  • Signs of a full trap: slow sink drainage, a sour smell, grease at the cleanout.
  • Lines to have checked: prep sink, three-compartment sink, mop sink and floor drains.
  • Records to keep: dates of every cleaning and who did it.

Backflow testing and why the water supply needs protecting

A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that stops water from flowing backward into the clean supply. Lawn irrigation, boilers, fire lines and some food service equipment use them.

A test shows whether the assembly's check valves and relief parts still hold. Which assemblies in Eagle, ID need testing, and how often, is set by your water utility or the authority that covers your property, so ask them before scheduling. The utility may be Veolia Water Idaho or the City of Eagle, depending on the address.

Where backflow devices commonly sit in small commercial buildings.
Location. Why it is there. What gets checked.
Irrigation line. Keeps sprinkler water out of the supply. Check valves and relief port.
Boiler or heating loop. Stops treated water backing up. Valve seals and pressure.
Fire line. Protects the supply from the fire system. Valve position and seats.
Food service equipment. Isolates carbonators and dish machines. Air gap or valve operation.

Hidden leaks in a commercial building

A leak in a shop or small office does not always show as a puddle. A water bill that climbs, a damp spot on a wall shared with a neighbor, or a floor that feels cool are all clues.

We use acoustic listening equipment and a moisture meter, and then a pressure test to isolate the line. Our leak detection page explains the tools in more depth. Detection finds the leak, and the repair is a separate step that may need your landlord or the utility.

Property managers, floor drains and lift pumps

Landlords and property managers often send work orders after a tenant calls. Tell us the unit, the problem and who can open the space, and keep the work order number handy when you call (208) 679-8357.

Floor drains in mechanical rooms and small lift pumps in below-grade restrooms are common service calls. We check the float, the check valve and the discharge line. If your building is in Garden City, our Garden City service area page covers that side.

What drives the cost of commercial plumbing work

What shapes the price.

  • Time of the call. After-hours and weekend calls take a plumber away from other plans, and that is reflected in the quote.
  • Access to the problem. A cleanout in a back room is fast. A drain under a finished floor or a lift pump pit takes longer.
  • Camera or locating work. Finding where the blockage or leak is may need a camera or listening gear before any repair.
  • Parts and equipment. Backflow assemblies, lift pump parts and commercial valves vary widely in price.
  • Building and lease rules. A landlord may need to approve the work, and some jobs need a permit through DOPL.

A commercial property policy usually responds to sudden accidental water damage and often excludes gradual leaks, wear and the failed part itself. Ask your agent about business interruption coverage, and check your lease for who is responsible for the building's pipes.

My restaurant floor drain keeps backing up. What should I do?
