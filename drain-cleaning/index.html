How we find what is blocking the drain

Drain cleaning starts with questions. Is it one fixture or several? Does the water drain slowly or not at all? Did a toilet gurgle when the sink emptied? The answers tell us whether the clog is in the trap, the branch line or the main.

One fixture that is slow points to a local clog. Several fixtures backing up together point to the branch or the main line. A drain that gurgles and smells can mean a partial blockage or a venting problem.

We then remove the trap or open a cleanout so the cable can enter the line. If the answer is not clear, we run a camera through the pipe and look at it. Drain cleaning in Eagle, ID is no different from anywhere else on this point. Cleaning without knowing what is down there is guesswork, and it is how a drain ends up clogged again a few weeks later.

A clogged drain: snake, cable or jet?

Each tool does a different job, and the wrong one wastes your money. The table lays out where each fits.

Which drain cleaning tool fits which clog.
Tool. Best for. Limit.
Hand auger (snake). Sink and tub traps, short clogs near the fixture. Short reach, pokes a hole through the clog.
Cable machine. Kitchen branch lines, tub lines, roots in small pipe. Cuts a channel and can leave grease on the pipe walls.
Cutter heads on cable. Roots, thick scale, hard buildup. Needs a pipe that is intact and a clear route.
Hydro jetter. Grease, soap film and silt along the whole pipe wall. Not for collapsed or broken pipe, and it needs a cleanout.
Camera. Finding the cause and checking the result. Shows the problem and does not clear it.

Drain snaking and cabling, step by step

A hand auger is a coiled steel cable with a crank. It works well for a trap or a few feet of pipe. A cable machine is a motorized drum that feeds a longer, stiffer cable through the line, and it handles the kitchen branch and bathroom lines a hand tool cannot reach.

  1. We pull the trap or open the nearest cleanout and lay down drop cloths.
  2. We feed the cable until it meets resistance, then rotate it so the head bites or breaks up the blockage.
  3. We pull the debris back out when possible and run water to check the flow.
  4. We camera or re-run the line if the clog has a history, so the pipe is clear and not only open.

A snake punches a hole, and the hole can close again. If grease is lining the pipe, the water will find the hole for a while and then the slow drain returns. That is the pattern that sends people back to the phone.

Why kitchen sink clogs keep coming back

Cooking grease is liquid when it leaves the pan and solid by the time it reaches the cooler pipe under the floor. It builds a layer that catches coffee grounds, rice and starch. The pipe narrows a little each month until a small plug stops it.

  • Wipe pans with a paper towel before they go in the sink, and put the towel in the trash.
  • Keep coffee grounds, eggshells and pasta out of the drain, even with a disposal.
  • Run hot water with a little dish soap for a minute after greasy dishes.
  • Skip the chemical cleaners, which can sit in a pipe and burn skin if the sink must be opened later.

If your kitchen line has clogged twice in a season, ask for a camera look and a jetting pass. It costs more than a single snake, but it can end the cycle. For a disposal that hums or leaks, see garbage disposal repair.

A drain line that leaks instead of clogging

What drives the price of drain cleaning

What shapes the price.

  • Where the clog sits. A trap clog near the fixture is quick. A blockage forty feet down a branch line takes more time and a longer cable.
  • Access. A cleanout makes the job simple. Without one, we pull a toilet or work from the roof vent, which adds labor and sometimes a reset of the fixture.
  • The tool the clog needs. A hand auger is the quickest step. A cutter head, a jetter or a camera adds time and equipment.
  • What the blockage is made of. Soft soap film clears easily. Roots, scale and grease that has hardened take more passes and sometimes a second visit.
  • Time of the call. Nights and weekends can be priced differently from a daytime appointment. We tell you before work starts.

Insurance for a clogged drain depends on what the water did. A sudden overflow that soaks a floor may be covered, while buildup and wear are normally treated as upkeep and the cleaning itself rarely is. Photograph everything before cleanup and read your policy.

Common questions

Do you handle drain cleaning emergencies at night or on weekends in Eagle?

Yes. A sink that backs up at dinner or a tub that fills at midnight can be called in to (208) 679-8357 at any hour, since dispatch runs 24/7. We book same-day appointments when available, and we never promise an arrival time. Ask the dispatcher where the schedule stands, and keep water off the line in the meantime.

My drain was snaked and clogged again a few weeks later. Why?

A snake clears a path but may leave grease and soap film on the pipe wall, and that coating catches new debris. A camera look and a jetting pass can clean the whole wall.

Is it safe to use chemical drain cleaner first?

We advise against it. It rarely clears a full blockage, it can sit in the pipe, and it is hazardous to anyone who has to open the trap afterward. Plain hot water with dish soap is the safer first step.

My tub drains slowly but the sink is fine. Is that the same problem?

Usually not. A slow tub is most often hair and soap in the trap or stopper, which a hand tool can clear. If the shower and tub both back up, the clog is likely in the branch.

Can you clear a floor drain or a drain grate in the garage or yard?

Yes. Floor drains and yard grates clog with silt and leaves. We cable them, and a camera shows where the line goes. Driveway culvert lines are often a different matter, so tell us what you see.

How do I check that a plumber is licensed in Idaho?

Anyone can check a plumber's licence with the Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses (DOPL) at 208-334-3233 or on its website. We recommend checking any contractor before they open your walls.
