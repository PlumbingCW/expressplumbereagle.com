Emergency garbage disposal repair: a leak under the sink or a smell of burning

A leak under the sink will soak the cabinet base, swell particle board and run into the wall. A burning smell or a buzzing motor that will not stop means the unit is overheating. Both need a call. A disposal that merely runs slowly can wait for a same-day appointment when available.

While you wait for us.

Turn the disposal switch off, then unplug it or flip its breaker if you can reach it dry. Never put a hand into the disposal, even when it seems dead.

Empty the cabinet, set a bucket under the leak, and close the two valves under the sink only if the leak is on the supply side. If the water is coming from the drain, just stop running the sink and dishwasher.

The hum with no spin: what it means and how to clear it

A humming disposal means power reaches the motor but the grinding plate cannot turn. A spoon, bone, fruit pit or plastic piece is stuck against it. Most disposals have a hex socket in the center of the underside, and a hex wrench worked back and forth frees the plate.

  1. Switch the disposal off at the wall and unplug it or flip the breaker.
  2. Look down the drain with a flashlight and pull any object out with tongs, never fingers.
  3. Work a hex wrench in the underside socket back and forth until the plate turns freely.
  4. Press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit.
  5. Restore power, run cold water and test the unit.

If the reset button trips again at once, the motor may be failing and the unit is likely at the end of its life. Do not keep resetting it.

Finding a leak under the disposal

Dry the unit and the pipes with a towel, run water, and watch where the first drop forms. Most leaks come from one of four places, which the table lays out.

Where disposal leaks start and what fixes them.
Leak location. Likely cause. Fix.
Top, at the sink flange. Dried plumber's putty or loose mounting ring. Reseat the flange with new putty.
Side, at the dishwasher hose. Loose hose clamp or cracked fitting. Tighten or replace the hose and clamp.
Drain outlet to the trap. Worn gasket or loose flange. Replace the gasket and tighten.
Bottom or body seam. Cracked housing or failed seal. Replace the disposal.

A leak from the body of the unit cannot be repaired, since the housing is sealed. That is the one case where replacement is not optional.

Repair or replace: how we decide

Jams, loose connections and bad gaskets are repairs. A seized motor, a cracked body, or a unit that keeps tripping the reset means replacing it. We check the label for the model and age, because a long-running unit with worn bearings is not worth more parts.

Replacement involves some choices. A hardwired disposal connects to the circuit directly, while a corded one plugs into an outlet under the sink. Horsepower sets how well it grinds, and more power is not always needed for a small household. If you are adding a dishwasher, the disposal inlet has a knockout plug that must be removed before the hose connects, or the dishwasher cannot drain.

A like-for-like disposal swap is simple. Anything beyond it, such as new drain runs, is a question for DOPL, which issues Idaho plumbing permits. Electrical work follows its own rules.

The kitchen sink that backs up with the disposal

A disposal grinds food into small pieces, but it does not move them down the drain. Grease and starch still settle in the line and cause the sink to back up. If the water stands in the basin even with the disposal running, the clog is past the unit, and drain cleaning is the next step.

Restaurant kitchens around Heritage Park in Downtown Eagle use grease handling a home sink does not have, so do not treat your disposal like one. Two small details matter here. A dishwasher drain needs an air gap or high loop so dirty water cannot flow back into the dishwasher. If the dishwasher leaks onto the floor, check that connection.

What decides the cost of garbage disposal repair

What shapes the price.

  • Repair or replacement. A jam is quick work. A replacement adds the unit, the mounting hardware and the electrical hookup.
  • Unit size and type. Horsepower and build quality change the price of a new disposal.
  • Wiring. Hardwired and corded units connect differently, and some kitchens need a new outlet or switch.
  • Dishwasher connection. Opening the knockout plug and fitting a new dishwasher hose adds a step.
  • Condition of the drain. Corroded pipes or a clogged trap under the unit add parts and time.

A leaking disposal can soak a sink base fast. Insurers tend to treat a sudden pipe or seal failure differently from a slow seep that went unnoticed for weeks, and the worn-out disposal is usually yours to replace. Check the policy before the cabinet comes apart. We cannot promise coverage.
