What Hydro Jetting a Kitchen Sink Line Costs in Eagle, ID

Written by Eagle Express Plumbing. Published October 15, 2026.

Hydro jetting a kitchen sink line means cleaning the drain pipe with a high-pressure water hose and a nozzle, so the grease and scale come off the pipe wall instead of just getting poked through. The price in Eagle, ID depends on five things: how we reach the pipe, how long the run is, how heavy the buildup is, whether a camera is needed, and how much time the job takes.

We do not post a number here because a kitchen line can be a ten-foot stub or a long run to a cleanout across the house. What you can do is learn what goes into a quote, so you can compare two of them fairly.

The five things that set the price

  1. Access. A cleanout near the sink line lets the jetter hose go in directly. With no cleanout, the plumber may have to pull a trap, work from a roof vent, or find another way in, and that costs time.
  2. Length of the line. A short run from the kitchen to the main stack takes less time than a long horizontal run to a lateral on the far side of the house.
  3. How much grease is in the pipe. A thin film clears in one pass. A thick, hardened coating takes multiple passes at different nozzle settings.
  4. Camera work. Looking inside before and after tells you what is there, and it adds time and equipment to the visit.
  5. Time and conditions. Night, weekend and holiday calls, and tight spaces under a slab or in a crawlspace, change the labor.

The restaurants and shops around Heritage Park in Downtown Eagle are a useful contrast. A commercial kitchen there sends far more grease down its line than a family kitchen does, so it needs jetting on a schedule. A home line in Eagle, ID usually needs it only when it starts to clog again.

Homes in Banbury Meadows, built in phases from 1994 to 2019, also show how much line age varies. A kitchen from the first phase and one from the last may share a street, and the older line may hold more buildup. Ask the plumber to look at yours, not at the neighborhood.

None of these are tricks. They are the same factors every plumber weighs, and a good quote names which ones apply to your line.

Jetting versus snaking a kitchen line

A snake, or drain cable, is a rotating steel cable that bores through a plug. A jetter uses water pressure from a nozzle that points forward and backward, so it cuts the buildup and flushes it away.

Snake and jetter on a kitchen line.

A kitchen drain that was snaked and clogged again a few weeks later usually has a coating problem. That is where the extra cost of a jet can pay off, because you buy a clean pipe and not a hole.

Why the cleanout decides half the job

A cleanout is a capped fitting on the drain line, often in a crawlspace, garage, basement or outside near the house wall. It is the plumber's door into the pipe. If one sits close to the kitchen line, the job is simpler and the quote reflects it.

If your home has no usable cleanout, tell us when you call. We may suggest adding one, since that gives every future visit a clean entry point. A cleanout installation is a separate line on a quote, and a licensed plumber pulls any permit that applies.

Why a camera look belongs in the quote

Jetting a pipe that has collapsed or separated is a good way to make a bad problem worse. Water at high pressure can wash soil out of a broken joint and turn a small break into a sinkhole in the yard. A camera run first shows whether the pipe is sound enough to jet.

The camera also tells us what is inside, whether grease, scale, roots, or a belly where water sits. That changes which nozzle we choose and how many passes the job takes. A quote that has no camera step is a quote that is guessing.

Comparing jetting quotes without guessing

  • Does it say jetting or snaking? They are not the same job.
  • Does it include a camera look before the jet?
  • Does it name where the plumber will enter the pipe?
  • Is the price for one pass, a set time, or the whole line?
  • Does it cover night or weekend rates, if that applies to your call?
  • Does it say what happens if the camera shows a broken pipe?

Two quotes that look far apart are often quotes for two different jobs. Ask each plumber to describe the work in plain words, and the gap usually explains itself.

What grease does to a kitchen line, and why it returns

Hot grease is liquid when it leaves the pan. It cools in the pipe and sticks to the wall, and soap and food bits stick to it. Over months the opening gets narrower, until a slow drain turns into a stopped one.

After a jet, the best thing you can do is keep fat out of the sink. Wipe pans with a paper towel and put the towel in the trash. A cleaned line stays clean far longer than one that keeps taking in cooking oil, and it cuts down how often you need drain cleaning again.

When jetting is the wrong tool

A collapsed section, a bellied pipe full of water, a pipe broken by roots, and a wad of a foreign object all need a different fix. The camera will show which one you have. In those cases we would recommend hydro jetting only after the repair, or not at all.

A one-time clog from a dropped item is also usually a snake job, not a jet job. We tell you that on the call, and we would rather keep the quote small than sell the bigger tool.

Common questions

How much does hydro jetting a kitchen sink line cost?

The number depends on access, line length, grease buildup, camera work and time. We give a clear price before work starts after we hear about your line. Call (208) 679-8357.

Is hydro jetting better than snaking for a kitchen sink?

For a grease-coated line or a drain that clogs again, yes, because it cleans the pipe wall. For a single soft clog, a snake can be enough.

Do you need a cleanout to jet my kitchen line?

A cleanout makes it easier, and it helps keep the quote down. Without one, we may enter through a trap or a vent, or suggest installing a cleanout.

Can jetting damage my pipes?

Sound pipe handles it well. Pipes that are cracked, collapsed or badly corroded can be damaged, which is why we run a camera first.

Who is responsible for a clog, me or the Eagle Sewer District?

The district looks after the public main. Your kitchen line and the pipe to the main are yours in most cases, so ask the Eagle Sewer District where its responsibility ends. If a clog is in the main, call the district.

Do landlords in Eagle, ID pay for jetting a tenant's kitchen drain?

That depends on your lease and the cause. Grease dumped by a tenant is treated differently from a line that has failed with age, so read the lease and keep the work order.
