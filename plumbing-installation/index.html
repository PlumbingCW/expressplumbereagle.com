Plumbing Installation in Eagle, ID

Plumbing installation is the work of putting in new supply lines, drains, vents, fixtures and appliance connections, then testing them before anyone relies on them. What a plumbing installation job actually includes

A fixture swap is the simple end of the range. A remodel is the middle, where walls open and lines move. New construction is the far end, where every pipe is laid out before drywall exists.

Across all three, the sequence is the same: plan the routes, set shutoffs, run supply and drain lines, vent them, pressure test, then set the fixtures. Skipping the test is where leaks behind finished walls come from.

  • Toilets, sinks, faucets, tubs and showers.
  • Dishwasher, washing machine and ice maker hookups.
  • Garbage disposal and water heater connections.
  • Hose bibs and outdoor spigots.
  • Added bathrooms, laundry rooms and wet bars.

Rough-in, set-in and final: the three stages of a build

Plumbing work on new construction in Eagle, ID happens in stages, whether the lot sits near Eagle Road or out toward Star. The first stage is the rough-in, when pipes go in before walls close. The second is top-out and testing, and the last is the final, when fixtures and trim get set.

What happens at each stage.
Stage. What gets done. What gets checked.
Rough-in. Drain, vent and supply pipe placed in framing. Slope, support, fitting alignment.
Pressure test. Lines filled and held under pressure. Gauge holds with no drop.
Trim and set. Fixtures, faucets and appliances connected. Shutoffs, leaks at every joint.
Final. Water on, drains run, fixtures cycled. Flow, drainage and noise.

A gauge that drops during the hold means a leak, and it gets found while walls are still open. That is the easiest time to find one.

Gas piping: safety before any plumber visit

Gas lines for a range, dryer, fireplace or water heater are part of some installations. If you ever smell gas, that is a safety issue first and a plumbing job second.

If you smell gas, do this before you call a plumber.

Get everyone out right away, including pets, and leave the door open as you go. Call Intermountain Gas on its 24-hour line, 800-548-3679, from outside and away from the leak, and call 911 for the fire department.

Do not use a phone inside the building, flip light switches, use plugs or garage door openers, open or close windows, smoke or light matches. Do not go back in until you are told it is safe.

Intermountain Gas is the gas supplier for Eagle, ID, and the meter and service line up to it are the utility's concern. Once the utility clears the home, a licensed plumber can pressure test and install new gas piping. We never promise a gas response time, and we do not touch a line that smells like gas until the utility has made the building safe.

Permits in Idaho: DOPL, not the city

In Idaho the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses (DOPL) issues plumbing permits, and a city or county building permit cannot replace one. People who live in the house, primary or secondary, can buy a permit for their own work. Commercial buildings are excluded.

A DOPL permit lapses a year (365 days) after you buy it or after its last inspection, and inspections are requested by phone or on the eTRAKiT website. The state code is the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code. Across Ada County, how far a rule reaches differs by project, so ask DOPL at 208-334-3233, which can also confirm any plumber's licence.

What drives the cost of a plumbing installation

What shapes the price.

  • Distance to existing lines. Fixtures near current supply and drain lines are quick. A new bathroom on the far side of the house needs long runs.
  • Wall and floor access. Open framing is simpler than finished walls, slabs and ceilings.
  • Fixture and appliance choice. Specialty faucets, freestanding tubs and steam showers can require parts and rough-in work beyond standard.
  • Permit and inspection. A DOPL permit and inspection add a step to the schedule.
  • Gas, vent or drain changes. Moving a vent stack, adding gas or upsizing a drain adds labor and testing.

Installation is planned work, so homeowners insurance generally does not pay for it. Policies respond to sudden accidental damage and often exclude gradual leaks and wear. If a new fixture leaks later and damages floors, check your policy and the installer's terms.

Common questions

Do I need a permit for plumbing installation in Eagle, ID?

DOPL issues them, not the city. Owners can buy one for their own residence, and rules differ by job, so ask DOPL at 208-334-3233.

What do I do if I smell gas while a project is underway?

Get everyone out, call Intermountain Gas at 800-548-3679 from outside, and call 911. Do not use switches or phones inside the building.

Can you hook up a gas stove or convert one to propane?

A licensed plumber can connect a gas range once the supply is verified. Propane conversion depends on the appliance maker's kit, so we check the manual first.

Can you install a bathtub or shower in a remodel?

Yes, and the main question is whether the drain and valve rough-in match the fixture. We check that before ordering anything.

Is new construction plumbing different from remodel plumbing?

The materials match. The difference is access: new construction has open framing, and a remodel works around finished walls and existing lines.

Does a homeowner permit cover a commercial building in Eagle, ID?

No. The homeowner permit applies to your own primary or secondary residence. Commercial work needs a licensed plumber.
