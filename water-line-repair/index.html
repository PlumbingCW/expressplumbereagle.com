Emergency Water Line Repair in Eagle, ID

Water line repair in Eagle, ID deals with the buried pipe that carries water from the meter to your house. When that pipe leaks or breaks, you see it as weak pressure, a soft wet patch in the yard, or a meter that keeps turning.

A failing service line in Ada County is an urgent problem because it can wash out soil under a driveway or walkway. Our 24/7 dispatch line, (208) 679-8357, takes calls overnight and on weekends, and in the meantime you can close the home's main valve and watch the meter.

When a water line failure is an emergency

Call right away if water is surfacing in the yard, pooling along the foundation, or running with no pressure at all indoors. A split pipe inside the house is a different job, covered under burst pipe repair. A line that has broken under pressure can empty a lot of water into the ground without a sound. Slower signs, such as a lawn patch that stays green and soggy, can usually wait for a same-day appointment when available.

While you wait for us.

Close the main valve inside the house, then check whether the water in the yard slows or stops. If it keeps coming, the break is on the utility side of the meter, and that is something to report to your water supplier at once.

Note the meter reading, take a photo of the wet area, and keep kids and pets off soft ground near a driveway or slab. Do not dig. Call 811 first, because gas, power and communication lines also run through yards.

Who owns which part: Veolia, the City of Eagle or a private well

In Eagle, ID, water arrives from Veolia Water Idaho, from the City of Eagle's own system, or from a private well in some outlying areas. Which one serves you depends on the address, and your bill will tell you. Ask that utility where its responsibility ends, because we will not guess where the line of ownership falls.

That answer decides who digs. A break on the utility side of the meter is theirs to repair. A break between the meter and the house is generally a plumbing job we can quote. On a private well, the line from the pressure tank to the house is yours from end to end.

How we confirm the line is leaking before we dig

We never start with a shovel. Digging a guess costs more than any test. First we confirm the leak is on the service line, then we narrow where.

  1. Shut off every fixture, note the meter reading, wait two hours with no water use, and read it again. The EPA says that if the meter moves, you probably have a leak.
  2. Close the home's main valve and watch the meter. If it still moves, the leak is between the meter and your valve. If it stops, the leak is inside the house.
  3. Put a pressure gauge on a hose bib to see whether pressure holds or sags.
  4. Use acoustic listening gear and a pipe locator along the path to find the hiss and the line's route.
  5. Mark the spot, call 811, and plan the repair.

Low water pressure: a bad line or something simpler?

Weak pressure does not always mean a broken service line. Many causes sit inside the house and cost far less to fix. Use the table below to sort the symptom before you assume the worst.

Low pressure symptoms and the first thing to check.
What you notice. Likely cause. First check.
Low pressure at every fixture. Partly closed main valve or failing pressure regulator. Open the main fully and read a gauge at a hose bib.
Low on hot only. Water heater valve or sediment. Compare hot and cold at one faucet.
One fixture weak. Clogged aerator or cartridge. Unscrew the aerator and look.
Pressure fell slowly over months. Corroded galvanized line. Look at pipe material at the main.
Sudden drop plus a wet yard. Service line break. Meter test and call us.

Open trench or trenchless: what decides the method

A buried line can be replaced two ways. An open trench exposes the whole pipe and lets us check the bedding and fittings. Trenchless methods pull or push a new pipe through the old path with small pits at each end, which saves a driveway or a mature lawn and trees.

Trenchless is not always possible. Pipe condition, soil, depth, bends and other buried lines decide it, and we will say so after we see the route. Plain open-cut is often the right call for a short run where a trench is easy.

Water main, lead pipe and no-water questions

Replacing the water main in the street is the utility's work, not ours. If your neighbors lost water too, call the supplier. If only your house is dry, check the main valve, then call us.

Many homeowners ask whether their line is lead. We cannot tell from the street, and we will not claim anything about lines in Eagle. Ask your utility for any record of your service line material, and look at the pipe where it enters your house near the main valve.

What decides the cost of a water line repair

What shapes the price.

  • Length and depth of the line. Longer, deeper runs mean more excavation, more pipe and more time, whether open-cut or trenchless.
  • What sits on top of it. Lawn, concrete, pavers and plantings all change the work needed to open the ground and put it back.
  • Pipe material and size. The new line must match your supply, and different pipe types have different fittings and costs.
  • Spot repair or full replacement. One clean break can be a spot repair. A line with several failures often makes more sense replaced.
  • Permits and utility coordination. Permits for this work run through DOPL, and what applies varies, so ask them. Work at the meter may need your utility.

Policies handle buried lines unevenly. Some treat the service line separately or exclude it as wear, so ask your insurer before work starts. We cannot promise coverage.

Why is my water pressure suddenly low?
